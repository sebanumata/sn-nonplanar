The difference

It's not a slicer. Here, you design the toolpath.

A slicer takes your part and decides on its own how to fill it with layers.
sn_non-planar works the other way around: the line the nozzle draws is the design. You choose the pattern, the rhythm and the height of every wave, and that exact path is what gets printed.

With a slicer

  • You have to model the texture into the mesh: heavy files with millions of triangles.
  • The slicer cuts it into flat layers and approximates it as best it can.
  • If it isn't a solid, it can't print it: open cells, blobs, strands in mid-air.
  • Every layer has its own seam and its own breaks.

With sn_non-planar

  • You start from a simple shape and the toolpath adds the pattern.
  • The nozzle really moves up and down within the same loop.
  • You print things that only exist as a toolpath: open lattice, blobs, bands.
  • The wall comes out as one continuous line, with no breaks and no retractions.

What you get

Pieces nobody else makes

If you sell planters, lamps or decor, you get a finish no slicer can produce. That's your edge.

You design, not configure

You decide how the surface looks with design criteria: rhythm, amplitude, openness. No fighting hundreds of settings to get it "more or less" right.

Recipes already printed

The presets come from parts that were actually printed, not from theory. And when something turns out great, you save it as your own.

It's not a general-purpose slicer: it isn't meant for mechanical parts or for slicing any STL. It's a tool for designing the toolpath.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does it work with my Bambu Lab?
Yes, if it's single-nozzle: A1 mini, A1, A2L, P1P, P1S, X1, X1 Carbon, X1E, P2S or H2S. The A1 mini is the one I've tested the most; on the others, watch the machine during your first print.
How is it different from a slicer with a non-planar mode?
Those slicers start from your part and bend some layers, mostly the top ones. sn_non-planar starts from the toolpath: you design the wall pattern, line by line. That's why it does things a slicer can't, like lattice with open cells or blobs.
Do I need to know how to code or edit G-code?
No. Everything is done from a panel in Blender, with sliders and a preview of the toolpath.
Can I use it with other brands?
Not for now. I focused on Bambu Lab so every file comes out tested and ready to print.
